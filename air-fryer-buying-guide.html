How to Choose an Air Fryer

A practical guide to capacity, basket style, controls, cleanup, and the features that matter most.

Updated November 2026 · Independent educational guide

## Start with the way you cook Think about the meals you actually make. A compact basket model can suit one or two people, while a larger household may appreciate a wider basket or dual-zone design. Check the usable cooking area—not just the advertised quart capacity—because shape affects what fits. ## Basket or oven style? Basket air fryers are usually compact and straightforward to shake or turn. Oven-style models offer shelves and may handle toast or multiple foods at once, but they take more counter space and can require rotating trays for even results. ## Features worth comparing - **Capacity and footprint:** Measure your counter and storage space. - **Temperature range:** A broad range gives more flexibility. - **Controls:** Choose simple dials or digital presets you will actually use. - **Cleaning:** Removable, dishwasher-safe parts can save time; check the manual. - **Noise and alerts:** Consider whether beeps or fan noise matter in your kitchen. ## A simple decision Choose the smallest model that comfortably handles your usual portions. Extra capacity is useful only if you have room to store it and will use it.

How to Choose a Blender

Understand jar size, motor design, controls, cleaning, and the difference between personal and full-size blenders.

Updated November 2026 · Independent educational guide

## Match the blender to the job For smoothies on the go, a personal blender may be enough. For family-sized batches, soups, sauces, or crushing ice, compare full-size models and their stated capabilities. Do not assume a high wattage number alone guarantees better results. ## Jar material and capacity Glass resists staining and odors but is heavier and can break. Plastic is lighter; look for clear information about its material and care. Choose a capacity that suits your usual batch size without requiring you to fill the jar to the brim. ## Features to check - A stable base and secure lid - Useful low/high or variable-speed control - Pulse function for short bursts - Dishwasher-safe parts, if important to you - Availability and cost of replacement jars or lids ## Before you buy Read the manual for limits on hot liquids, ice, and continuous run time. Compare the warranty and whether the manufacturer sells replacement parts. A simple model that handles your regular recipes is often the best value.

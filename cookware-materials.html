Cookware Materials Compared

A plain-language overview of stainless steel, nonstick, cast iron, and carbon steel.

Updated November 2026 · Independent educational guide

## Stainless steel Stainless steel is versatile and durable when cared for properly. It can handle browning and many cooking methods, but food may stick if the pan is not preheated and used with suitable fat and temperature. ## Nonstick Nonstick cookware can make eggs and delicate foods easier to release and can simplify cleanup. Follow the maker's utensil and temperature guidance, avoid overheating empty pans, and replace cookware when the coating is damaged. ## Cast iron Cast iron retains heat well and can move from stovetop to oven when the pan's handle and seasoning allow. It is heavy and needs drying and care to reduce rust. Enameled cast iron has different care needs. ## Carbon steel Carbon steel is common in skillets and woks. Like bare cast iron, it generally needs seasoning and careful drying. It can be lighter than cast iron but still takes some maintenance. ## How to choose Think about the foods you cook, weight, maintenance, stovetop compatibility, and oven-safe temperature. Many kitchens benefit from a small mix of materials rather than one complete matching set.

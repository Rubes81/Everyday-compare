Robot Vacuum Buying Guide

What to know about navigation, thresholds, mapping, maintenance, docks, and pet hair.

Updated November 2026 · Independent educational guide

## Know what a robot can—and cannot—do Robot vacuums are designed for maintenance cleaning, not necessarily to replace every deep clean. Cables, small objects, thick rugs, and high thresholds can interrupt a cleaning run. Tidy floors improve results. ## Navigation and mapping Some models use systematic mapping; others use simpler movement patterns. Mapping can help with room selection and no-go zones, but setup quality and app support matter. Check whether maps can be saved for multiple floors if your home needs that. ## Dock and maintenance A self-emptying dock reduces how often you empty the bin, but bags or filters may add ongoing cost. Brushes, wheels, sensors, and filters still need regular cleaning. Check replacement-part availability. ## Pet hair and mixed flooring Look for brush designs that manage hair and confirm the model supports your floor types. Read owner feedback for the specific carpet or rug conditions similar to yours. ## Before choosing Measure thresholds, note rug types, check app compatibility, and compare warranty support. A robot that fits your layout is more useful than one with the longest feature list.

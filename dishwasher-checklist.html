Dishwasher Buying Checklist

Compare dimensions, rack layout, noise ratings, cycles, drying, and installation requirements.

Updated November 2026 · Independent educational guide

## Measure before comparing features Confirm the opening width, height, and depth, and check door clearance. Built-in dishwashers commonly have standard dimensions, but verify your actual space and plumbing/electrical requirements. Installation may require a qualified professional. ## Rack layout matters Adjustable upper racks, fold-down tines, and a third rack can help—but only if they fit the dishes and cookware you use. Look at interior photos and measure tall items such as bottles or sheet pans. ## Noise and cycles Manufacturers publish noise ratings in decibels. Lower numbers are generally quieter, though installation and room acoustics also affect what you hear. Compare normal, heavy, quick, and sanitizing cycles based on your needs. ## Drying and efficiency Check how the model dries plastics and whether it uses a heated element, fan, or other method. Review the EnergyGuide label and estimated annual energy use. ## Final checks Confirm delivery, haul-away, installation, warranty, and return terms. A dishwasher with a sensible rack layout and reliable service support is a better fit than a long list of rarely used settings.

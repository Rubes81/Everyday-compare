Vacuum Buying Checklist for Homes with Pets

Compare brush design, filtration, bin size, upholstery tools, maintenance, and floor compatibility.

Updated November 2026 · Independent educational guide

## Consider where pet hair collects Hair may gather on rugs, hard floors, stairs, furniture, and pet beds. Think about the surfaces you clean most often and choose a vacuum head and attachments suited to those areas. ## Brush rolls and hair management Some brush rolls are designed to reduce hair wrap, but no design is maintenance-free. Check how easily the brush roll can be removed and cleaned, and whether replacement parts are sold. ## Filtration and emptying If dust control matters in your home, review the filtration specification and how the vacuum seals around its filter and bin. Emptying can release dust, so consider the bin design and whether bags are preferable for your household. ## Attachments that help A motorized upholstery tool can help with furniture; a crevice tool can reach edges and car interiors. Check which tools are included rather than assuming they come in the box. ## Final checklist Compare weight, cord length or battery runtime, bin capacity, filter costs, warranty, and service support. Choose based on your actual floors and cleaning routine—not just a “pet” label.
